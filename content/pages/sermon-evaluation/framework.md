# Sermon Evaluation Framework

## Purpose and theological posture

An effective sermon does more than transfer doctrinal data. It uncovers the divine purpose of the biblical passage and weds that purpose to the real, shared condition of the congregation. The sermon must speak with biblical authority because Scripture is God's inspired, inerrant, and infallible Word, while the preacher exercises that authority as a servant who remains under the same Word with the hearers.

This framework calls that posture **delegated authority with shared subjection**. The preacher should proclaim Christ's standard without presenting himself as the mastered example standing above the congregation. He should credibly receive and apply the text as a fellow sinner, sufferer, and dependent recipient of Christ's grace. This does not require autobiographical confession in every sermon: shared subjection may be implicit, and personal disclosure must remain proportionate, prudent, and Christward.

The evaluation therefore attends to seven ministry priorities:

1. **Textual Purpose and Fidelity** – Does the sermon mirror the passage's burden and trajectory?
2. **FCF Precision** – Is the fallen condition concrete, text-tethered, and determinative for the sermon?
3. **Christ-Centered Resolution** – Does the gospel resolve the identified need organically?
4. **Transformational Application** – Are applications specific, differentiated, heart-and-life oriented, and grace-driven?
5. **Structural Cohesion** – Do the proposition, points, transitions, and conclusion serve one purpose?
6. **Doctrinal Fidelity** – Does the sermon affirm rather than contradict implicated core doctrines while treating secondary and tertiary differences proportionately and charitably?
7. **Pastoral Posture** – Does the preacher use delegated authority with shared subjection, courageous gentleness, ethical influence, and care for differently situated hearers?

## Two-step evaluation

### Step 1 – Descriptive extraction and semantic evidence

Step 1 reconstructs what the preacher actually did before any scores are assigned. It identifies the Scripture introduction, sermon introduction, proposition, main points, explanations, illustrations, applications, conclusion, Fallen Condition Focus, general comments, and extraction confidence.

It also collects structured evidence for:

- Pastoral self-application and shared need.
- Servant authority grounded in Scripture and Christ.
- Courageous and gentle care.
- Differentiated application to the rebellious, repentant, weak, doubting, suffering, and victimized.
- Pastoral use or misuse of power.
- Core doctrines implicated by the sermon, affirming evidence, contradicting evidence, and the handling of secondary and tertiary doctrine.
- Illustration ethics, including dignity, confidentiality, consent, anonymization, and treatment of vulnerable people.

Evidence is semantic and context-sensitive. The evaluator must not substitute pronoun counts, required phrases, English keyword lists, or performative confession for actual meaning. When audio is available, evidence should include approximate timestamps; otherwise it should quote or closely paraphrase the relevant passage.

### Step 2 – Analytical scoring and coaching

Step 2 receives the extraction and verifies it against the audio when available. It scores every rubric criterion with an integer from 1 to 5, provides section feedback, applies the core-doctrine gate, computes registry-defined aggregates, and produces coaching feedback.

## Evaluation methods

**Standard** uses one scoring run.

**Self-consistency** uses three independent scoring runs with different seeds. Administrators may choose one to nine runs. When multiple runs are used:

1. Numeric criterion scores are combined using confidence-weighted averaging.
2. The core-doctrine gate follows the confidence-weighted majority of independent judgments.
3. A feedback-only synthesis step summarizes consensus and preserves useful minority concerns.
4. The displayed score range describes variation among completed runs; it is not labeled statistical confidence or human-validated uncertainty.

`Extraction_Confidence` and `Scoring_Confidence` remain bounded model-reported values from 0 to 1. They describe the evaluator's assessment of input and scoring clarity; they do not prove correctness.

## Scoring scale

- **5 – Exemplary:** Exceptional, evidence-rich execution with no substantive improvement readily identifiable.
- **4 – Strong:** Clearly above a competent baseline, with only minor refinements needed.
- **3 – Adequate:** A competent midpoint with meaningful room for growth; this is not failure.
- **2 – Weak:** Present but thin, unclear, forced, inconsistent, or pastorally unsafe.
- **1 – Deficient:** Absent, inaccurate, misleading, contradictory, exploitative, or counterproductive.

For every score other than 3, the evaluator must be able to identify specific evidence. The schemas reject scores outside 1–5, confidence values outside 0–1, and unknown fields.

### A. Introduction

Goal: Orient hearers to the text, surface its burden, and awaken text-relevant attention.

Sub-Criteria:
1. **FCF Introduced** *(A specific fallen condition is derived from the preached text and previewed.)*
2. **Arouses Attention** *(The opening creates text-relevant tension or need rather than distracting from Scripture.)*
**Feedback:** Give concise, evidence-grounded, actionable coaching.

### B. Proposition

Goal: State one text-derived controlling truth that weds principle and response.

Sub-Criteria:
1. **Principle + Application Wed** *(Subject and complement form one gospel principle with an implied or explicit response.)*
2. **Establishes Main Theme** *(The proposition controls the sermon's scope and governs every point without competitors.)*
3. **Summarizes Introduction** *(The proposition carries forward and resolves the tension or need raised in the introduction.)*
**Feedback:** Give concise, evidence-grounded, actionable coaching.

### C. Main Points

Goal: Develop the proposition through clear, progressive, text-rooted movements.

Sub-Criteria:
1. **Clarity** *(Main points are succinct, memorable, and readily distinguishable.)*
2. **Hortatory Universal Truths** *(Points state timeless truths that call hearers to trust or obey rather than merely recapping verses.)*
3. **Proportional & Coexistent** *(Each point advances the single proposition through a distinct theological move toward a coherent climax.)*
4. **Exposition Quality** *(The sermon explains the text's meaning in context before moving to application.)*
5. **Illustration Quality** *(Illustrations illuminate the stated point and remain proportionate to it.)*
6. **Application Quality** *(Applications are specific, grace-motivated, and directed to heart and life.)*
**Feedback:** Give concise, evidence-grounded, actionable coaching.

### D. Exegetical Support

Goal: Demonstrate that the sermon is governed by the passage's meaning, context, and purpose.

Sub-Criteria:
1. **Alignment with Text** *(The sermon's structure and emphasis mirror the passage's burden.)*
2. **Handles Difficulties** *(Key interpretive, translation, and theological tensions are engaged honestly.)*
3. **Proof Accuracy & Clarity** *(Claims are supported with sound, understandable reasoning from the text.)*
4. **Context & Genre Considered** *(Literary, historical, canonical, and redemptive contexts are handled appropriately.)*
5. **Not Belabored** *(The sermon stops proving once sufficient and avoids pedantic overload.)*
6. **Aids Rather Than Impresses** *(Exegetical detail serves listener understanding rather than scholarly display.)*
**Feedback:** Give concise, evidence-grounded, actionable coaching.

### E. Application

Goal: Carry the text's purpose into concrete, grace-powered faith and obedience.

Sub-Criteria:
1. **Clear & Practical** *(Concrete next steps or heart postures are identifiable.)*
2. **Redemptive Focus** *(Application is motivated by Christ's person, work, and grace rather than bare willpower.)*
3. **Mandate vs Idea Distinction** *(Divine commands are distinguished from pastoral wisdom and suggestions.)*
4. **Passage Supported** *(Application flows organically from the passage's explained meaning.)*
**Feedback:** Give concise, evidence-grounded, actionable coaching.

### F. Illustrations

Goal: Use concrete material that clarifies truth without eclipsing it or exploiting people.

Sub-Criteria:
1. **Lived-Body Detail** *(Concrete, sensory realism helps hearers inhabit the truth.)*
2. **Strengthens Points** *(Illustrations illuminate the stated truth without hijacking focus.)*
3. **Proportion** *(Length and frequency are economical and avoid narrative domination.)*
4. **Illustration Ethics** *(Stories protect confidentiality, dignity, and vulnerable people; they do not exploit family, congregants, or counselees.)*
   - **5:** Consistently protects dignity and privacy, uses permission or sufficient anonymization, and never turns vulnerable people into sermon material.
   - **3:** No evident ethical breach, but privacy, permission, or power implications are unclear.
   - **1:** Exposes, humiliates, stereotypes, or exploits identifiable or vulnerable people, or uses entrusted information irresponsibly.
**Feedback:** Give concise, evidence-grounded, actionable coaching.

### G. Conclusion

Goal: Land the sermon with a faithful summary, gospel-rooted exhortation, and decisive end.

Sub-Criteria:
1. **Summary** *(The conclusion concisely recapitulates the proposition and main movements.)*
2. **Compelling Exhortation** *(The sermon closes with a specific, gospel-rooted call to response.)*
3. **Climax** *(The sermon reaches an appropriate theological and pastoral crescendo without emotional manipulation.)*
4. **Pointed End** *(The sermon lands decisively rather than fading or introducing new material.)*
**Feedback:** Give concise, evidence-grounded, actionable coaching.

### H. Doctrinal Fidelity

Goal: Guard the sermon from compensating for contradiction of essential Christian doctrine with strong homiletical form. This is a gate-only section and is not averaged into Overall Impact.

Sub-Criteria:
1. **Core Doctrine Fidelity** *(The sermon affirms and does not deny or materially distort the ministry's core doctrines when they are implicated by the text or sermon claims.)*
   - **5:** Core doctrine implicated by the sermon is stated accurately, specifically, and integrated with the passage.
   - **3:** No core contradiction is evident, but doctrinal treatment is generic, implicit, or not materially implicated.
   - **1:** The sermon explicitly denies or materially distorts an implicated core doctrine.
2. **Doctrinal Proportionality** *(The sermon emphasizes doctrine in proportion to the passage and does not force a system onto the text.)*
3. **Secondary & Tertiary Charity** *(Differences among faithful Christians are represented accurately, humbly, and according to their proper doctrinal weight.)*

Core Doctrine Gate:
- Set `Core_Doctrine_Gate` to `FAIL` only when the sermon provides specific evidence of an explicit denial or material distortion of a core doctrine implicated by its text or claims.
- Omission of a doctrine the passage does not implicate is not a failure. Ambiguity, thinness, or generic treatment should lower the score but remain `PASS` unless a contradiction is evidenced.
- Provide the exact claim and its location in `Gate_Reason` when failing the gate.
**Feedback:** Give concise, evidence-grounded, actionable coaching.

### I. Pastoral Posture and Humble Authority

Goal: Evaluate delegated authority with shared subjection: the preacher proclaims God's Word boldly while visibly remaining under it with the hearers.

Sub-Criteria:
1. **Shared Subjection & Self-Application** *(The preacher credibly receives and applies the text as one addressed by it, sharing the congregation's need for Christ and grace without making himself the center.)*
   - **5:** At material exhortations the preacher stands under the text, appropriately owns shared need or dependence, and directs hearers with himself to Christ.
   - **3:** Shared need is generic or implicit; the sermon is mainly hearer-directed but contains no clear self-exemption.
   - **1:** The congregation is treated as the deficient party while the preacher presents himself as observer, exception, superior, or accomplished standard.
2. **Servant Authority** *(Authority is located in Scripture and Christ rather than personality, office, experience, or preference; command is distinguished from counsel.)*
   - **5:** Speaks with confidence derived from the text while demonstrating submission to Christ, appropriate limits, and accountable servant leadership.
   - **3:** Generally biblical and non-domineering, though the delegated source or limits of authority remain implicit.
   - **1:** Claims authority from office, charisma, loyalty, or preference; binds consciences beyond Scripture or elevates the preacher.
3. **Courageous & Gentle Care** *(The preacher names sin and Christ's standard plainly with affection, patience, grief, hope, and gospel sufficiency.)*
   - **5:** Combines moral clarity with evident love, patience, hope, and dependence on Christ's provision.
   - **3:** Clear and generally kind but clinical, generic, or uneven in warmth and hope.
   - **1:** Harsh, contemptuous, mocking, coercive, manipulative, or so soft that biblical authority disappears.
4. **Differentiated Pastoral Application** *(Application distinguishes the rebellious, repentant, weak, doubting, suffering, and victimized rather than flattening every hearer into the same culpability or remedy.)*
   - **5:** Meaningfully differentiates hearers and applies law, comfort, warning, hope, and responsibility according to their condition.
   - **3:** Application is sound but mostly undifferentiated or addressed to a generic hearer.
   - **1:** Misassigns guilt, burdens sufferers or victims, comforts the unrepentant, or treats every condition as morally identical.
5. **Pastoral Use of Power** *(The sermon uses pastoral influence to serve truth and hearers, avoiding fear, humiliation, loyalty demands, retaliation, manipulation, and conscience-binding.)*
   - **5:** Uses influence transparently and protectively, invites biblical examination, and directs loyalty to Christ rather than the preacher or institution.
   - **3:** No evident abuse of power, though accountability, conscience boundaries, or invitations to examine the claims remain implicit.
   - **1:** Uses fear, shame, threats, public exposure, institutional loyalty, or spiritual authority to control hearers or silence legitimate examination.
**Feedback:** Give concise, evidence-grounded, actionable coaching.

### Weighting rationale

These weights balance two considerations: the ministry priorities represented by each aggregate and the breadth of the rubric questions used to measure it. They are not assigned mechanically from member count alone, because the number of questions in an aggregate is itself a rubric-design choice. The aggregate order reflects the intended priority: textual fidelity, application effectiveness, structure cohesion, pastoral posture, proposition clarity, illustrations, and introduction.

To keep a narrow aggregate from accidentally giving each of its questions more leverage than questions in higher-priority, broader aggregates, the selection also considers the effective weight assigned to each member in the composite: `aggregate weight / number of members`. A one-point increase in a member changes the 1–5 `Overall_Impact_Base` by the corresponding coefficient expressed as a decimal; for example, a member with 3.00% effective weight changes it by 0.03 points.

| Pastoral Posture and Humble Authority | 5 | 3.00% |

This distribution keeps textual fidelity and application effectiveness primary; places structure and pastoral posture next; gives pastoral posture and proposition clarity equal 3.00% influence per member while assigning pastoral posture the larger aggregate share because it covers five distinct concerns rather than three; and treats illustrations and introduction as supporting dimensions.

Every non-gate Step 2 rubric criterion appears in exactly one aggregate, so no weighted criterion is omitted or double-counted. Doctrinal Fidelity remains intentionally outside the weighted average and is enforced through the non-compensatory doctrine gate.

The weights should be revisited if aggregate membership changes or human calibration data shows that the composite no longer reflects the intended ministry priorities.

`Overall_Impact_Base` is the weighted average of the seven aggregates above.

`Doctrinal_Fidelity` is not weighted. If `Core_Doctrine_Gate = FAIL`, `Overall_Impact` is capped at 3.0; strong homiletical form cannot compensate for contradiction of an implicated core doctrine.

When the optional duration adjustment is enabled, the selected `Overall_Impact` may then be reduced by the configured duration penalty. Duration policy is reported separately and is never supplied to homiletical coaching. When it is disabled, no hypothetical penalty is computed or exposed.

## Optional sermon-length adjustment

The duration adjustment is optional and disabled by default. When enabled, the selected Overall Impact receives a deterministic penalty outside the configured 35–50 minute range, capped at one point. Duration is reported separately in metadata and never supplied to homiletical coaching. When disabled, the system does not compute or expose a hypothetical penalty.

## Coaching and reports

The evaluator produces:

- Aggregate coaching for every weighted dimension plus doctrinal fidelity.
- A concise overall coaching summary.
- Specific strengths, growth areas, and next steps.
- Step 1 extraction and evidence.
- Step 2 section and criterion scores.
- Markdown, JSON, CSV, and PDF-ready report artifacts.

Report metadata—generation time, model, extraction confidence, scoring confidence, evaluation methodology, audio duration, and duration-policy state—appears at the end so that the aggregate table begins on the first report page.
